# Hi Hat brand guidelines

Hi Hat helps discerning listeners discover and hear music with clarity. The identity should feel calm, precise, cultural, and confident—not like a generic streaming service.

The visual overview is [hi-hat-brand-kit-v1.png](hi-hat-brand-kit-v1.png).

## Core idea

The mark combines two hi-hat cymbal discs, a soundwave, and a narrow listening/pause gap. It represents the moment mechanical playback becomes attentive listening.

Use the complete mark for presentations and campaigns. At small sizes, use the reduced cymbal-and-wave symbol. Preserve its proportions and negative space; do not redraw it as a music note or headphones.

## Voice

- Direct, quiet, and specific.
- Prefer short action labels and plain language.
- Avoid hype, vague lifestyle claims, and technical jargon that does not help listeners.
- Campaign line: **Hear what matters.**

## Palette

| Token | Value | Primary use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dark | `#141413` | Brand canvas, primary dark text |
| Light | `#FAF9F5` | Brand paper, text on dark |
| Mid gray | `#B0AEA5` | Secondary copy and details |
| Light gray | `#E8E6DC` | Dividers and quiet surfaces |
| Orange | `#D97757` | Primary brand accent |
| Blue | `#6A9BCC` | Secondary data/accent role |
| Green | `#788C5D` | Tertiary data/accent role |

Use one dominant neutral pair and one accent in a composition. Orange is the default brand accent; blue and green differentiate secondary systems rather than decorate arbitrarily.

The product UI currently uses chartreuse as a functional playback signal. Keep that semantic product token distinct until a tested, product-wide migration replaces it; do not mix orange and chartreuse casually on one surface.

## Typography

- Headings at 24 pt or larger: Poppins, falling back to Arial.
- Body and editorial copy: Lora, falling back to Georgia.
- Dense native application controls may retain the platform system font for readability and rendering stability.
- Use tabular numerals for durations, bitrate, sample rate, and other changing measurements.

## Composition

- Use a clear grid, strong gutters, and generous negative space.
- Let one idea dominate each panel or screen.
- Prefer real product fragments, tactile music materials, and restrained analog texture.
- Use texture only in campaign imagery; keep operational UI surfaces clean.
- Keep copy sparse and readable. Never use fake tiny paragraphs.

## Avoid

- Generic notes, headphones, equalizer badges, or copied streaming-service motifs.
- Startup gradients, purple-blue AI glow, cheap neon, glass effects, or random accent colors.
- Inconsistent logo variants, stretched marks, crowded clear space, or effects that obscure the symbol.
- Decorative mockups that do not demonstrate a real application of the identity.

## Minimum governance

Before shipping a branded artifact, confirm that its mark is consistent, palette roles are intentional, type follows the heading/body split, contrast is readable, text is concise, and any product UI pictured is believable.
